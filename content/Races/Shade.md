# Description
A shade possesses a physical body that looks, acts, and feels similar to a living member of their original race, at least superficially. Shades must breathe, consume food and drink, and require shelter from adverse weather. Despite the functional similarities to a living body, a shade isn’t composed of flesh and blood. Their bodies are a memory of who they once were, inhabited and quickened by the presence of their soul. Exhaustion, deprivation, and injury take their toll on a shade’s body, disrupting their body’s ability to maintain the connection to their soul. 

The more injured or exhausted a shade, the less corporeal they appear. The colours of their body wash out to pale, desaturated tones, and light begins to pass through them. Blood from their wounds starts out as red and vibrant as any humanoid’s, but closer to death, their wounds cease to bleed and seem to evaporate at the edges.

# Brief History
 When a person dies, if their mind and will are strong enough, they may form into a shade. The strength of a shade’s memory of themselves and their place in life is the core of their being. Newer shades are almost impossible to distinguish from their prior, fully-living selves. Like anything exposed to the gnawing ravages of time, however, memory fades. The older a shade gets, the memories that keep them tethered to the living world begin to fray. They might grow forgetful, losing track of where they are or what year it is. Their memories might drift together, causing them to see people around them as figures from their past. 

# Rarity in the Union
Shades are exceedingly rare in the Union. It is a rare person that has a strong enough will to form one.

# Political Standing in the Union
Shades who can pass for the race they once were have the same political standing as that race. The Union has no official stance on individuals who have been outed as shades.

# Reputation in the Union
Shades who can pass as their original race share its reputation. Shades who have been outed are often shunned by members of their former race, but are often accepted by others.
