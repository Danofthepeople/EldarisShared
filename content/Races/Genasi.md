# Description
Genasi are a human-like race that has been infused with elemental power. The four prime elemental planes, earth, fire, water and air, are all repsresented in the genasi people. Their minds and bodies are often shaped by the element within them, this means that they tend to get on well with members of their own element, but may struggle with other genasi.

# Brief History
The elemental gods of [[Vokdall|fire]], [[Rhodon|earth]], [[Yrdis|water]] and [[Udona|air]], bend the weave around them with barely a thought. Each of these gods forge small sanctuaries in places of resonance with their element. These sanctuaries attract devout followers from all over Eldaris. Humans that make their home in these sanctuaries have their weave altered by the elemental resonance. 

All children born in these sanctuaries are born as genasi. Each genasi takes on the element of their god, and feel an uncanny connection to them and their domain. It is common for genasi to spend their entire lives at the point of resonance where they were born. Some however retain the adventurous human spirit, leading them to leave their homes, in search of new horizons. In the union genasi travellers are usually seen spreading the word of their god, while travelling from one resonance to another. Those that settle outside of the sanctuaries are few in number, but are always welcomed.

# Rarity in the Union
Genasi are rare in the union, as few genasi settle down there. However, as genasi are a stable race, meaning their children are also genasi, the population of genasi in the union is slowly growing. Notably second generation genasi, born outside of the sanctuaries often feel no desire to return their sanctuaries.

# Political Standing in the Union
Genasi are such a minor group in the union that they have little sway in any politics, though they usually benefit from their human heritage.

# Reputation in the Union
Genasi are met with an odd reverance in the union, and are usually seen as spiritual nomads. While they can hold any position in the union, most gravitate towards the clergy.
