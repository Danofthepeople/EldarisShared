# Description
Aasimar largely resemble humans, some are even indistinguishable from a human. However, most aasimar have something that sets them apart, precious metal that runs through cracks in their skin, skin that sparkles in the sunlight, eyes that hold beautiful vistas, iridescent hair that shifts colours as they move, all these things and more hint at an aasimars divine heritage.

# Brief History
Roughly 150 years ago, once every ten years on the sixth day of the year, [[Xemos]] would bless Eldaris with the Dawn of Ascension. On this joyous day, the skies filled with a beautiful aurora, [[Zuzarlra|Zuzarlra's]] fiends were banished, and new divines ascended. Humans born on this day were suffused with the divine energy flooding the weave, transforming them into aasimar. Each aasimar was born with an angelic guide, a celestial watcher that whispered through dreams, offering visions, prophecies, and feelings of guidance. However, as mortals, aasimar were never bound to good as angels were, their lives remained their own, shaped by their choices rather than divine will.

Yet, the Dawn of Ascension has not occurred in generations, and no new aasimar are known to have been born since. How aasimar come into existence now, if they do at all, is a mystery. The radiant souls of those who remain still carry the blessings of their celestial heritage, and they are often showered with reverence and expectation, regardless of their own desires. The world may no longer see the Dawn of Ascension, but the echoes of its divine touch still linger in those who once bore its light.

# Rarity in the Union
The aasimar are approaching extinction. There is currently no known method for an aasimar to be born, meaning that the youngest aasimar possible is over 150 years old.

# Political Standing in the Union
The few aasimar that remain share in the political strength of humanity.

# Reputation in the Union
Most of the short-lived races will never have seen an aasimar, for the long-lived races they would be a distant memory. Though if any were to see one of these last vestiges of light, undoubtedly they would be met with adulation.
